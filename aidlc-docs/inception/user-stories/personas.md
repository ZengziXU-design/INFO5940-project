# CourseCompass Personas

**Status:** Original personas approved at cd3ebd5. The timetable interaction revision was approved with Workflow Planning at f36a8ed.

**Basis:** [Vision](../../../docs/vision.md), [requirements](../requirements/requirements.md), and [user stories](stories.md).

These are illustrative roles based on the agreed planning goals. A Cornell student may fit more than one role, regardless of their major or degree program.

## P1 — Student planning around program requirements

- **Goal:** Meet a program requirement while building a feasible schedule with an appropriate credit load.
- **Context:** Consults the applicable official program page and identifies a class to include. Has completed-course information and one or more times when they cannot attend.
- **Challenge:** A required lecture may have a discussion or lab, and the compatible package may conflict with another requirement. Prerequisite or repeat eligibility may need clarification.
- **Success:** The student can confirm required courses, credits and time blocks, and verify how well the proposed schedule meets them. If a fixed course cannot be scheduled, they understand the issue before deciding whether to adjust the request.
- **Example:** “I need this program course and 12–15 credits. I cannot attend on Friday afternoon.”
- **Stories:** US-01, US-02, US-03, US-04, US-05, US-06, US-07, US-08, US-09, US-10, US-11, US-12.

## P2 — Student exploring electives across fields

- **Goal:** Explore an unfamiliar discipline without making the semester unmanageable.
- **Context:** Compares electives across Cornell subjects that have offerings for the selected term. Has a preferred weekly study budget and values schedule convenience.
- **Challenge:** Official descriptions give useful starting information, while workload estimates or reviews may be sparse, subjective or unavailable.
- **Success:** The student can start with a recommended timetable, inspect the evidence for an individual elective, and try a change that preserves confirmed restrictions.
- **Example:** “I would like a policy or design elective that fits around my other classes. I have about eight hours a week for study outside class.”
- **Stories:** US-01, US-02, US-03, US-04, US-05, US-06, US-07, US-08, US-09, US-10, US-12.

## P3 — Student seeking research or career relevance

- **Goal:** Find courses with depth in a chosen topic and balance them with the rest of the semester.
- **Context:** Can describe a research or career interest more readily than a list of individual course codes. May want to retain a particular course at the center of a schedule.
- **Challenge:** Apparent topical fit may come from summaries or instructor background, and relevant courses may have heavy combined workloads or conflicting times.
- **Success:** The student can see how topic fit and available evidence influenced recommendations, preserve a fixed course, and revise preferences without losing established constraints.
- **Example:** “Keep my required seminar. I want a strong theoretical elective and prefer a lighter overall workload.”
- **Stories:** US-01, US-02, US-03, US-04, US-05, US-06, US-07, US-08, US-09, US-10, US-11, US-12.

## P4 — Team member running the local demonstration

- **Goal:** Show the complete recommendation workflow in a repeatable local demo.
- **Context:** Runs the application on a team laptop with a known fixture or a model mock if a service becomes unavailable.
- **Challenge:** An external failure or missing source can disrupt a live demonstration or make the status of displayed data unclear.
- **Success:** The demo distinguishes fixture or mock results from live results and can reproduce the approved scenarios.
- **Stories:** US-13.
